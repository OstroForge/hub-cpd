Acute Asthma

JRCALC Asthma · oxygen · nebulisers · adrenaline

Teaching summary. Recheck the live JRCALC asthma page, local PGDs and stock before you treat. The March 2024 update revised asthma in adults and children.

Five key points to remember

1. Grade the attack. One life-threatening feature is enough.
2. A silent chest is pre-terminal, not a successful nebuliser.
3. Oxygen target 94–98%. The COPD 6-minute, 88–92% rule is a different guideline.
4. Salbutamol 5 mg, oxygen-driven. Add ipratropium 500 micrograms when the attack is severe.
5. Still deteriorating on continuous salbutamol: consider intramuscular adrenaline.

Grade it

Moderate

  • Talks in sentences
  • No severe feature
  • Still treat, and safety-net

Acute severe — any one

  • Cannot finish a sentence
  • Respiratory rate 25 or more
  • Heart rate 110 or more
  • Peak flow 33–50% of best

Life-threatening — any one

  • Sats under 92%
  • Silent chest, cyanosis, feeble effort
  • Exhaustion or altered consciousness
  • Hypotension or arrhythmia
  • Peak flow under 33%

Not the COPD rules

Oxygen-driven nebuliser Yes. Keep the saturation at 94–98%. Repeat salbutamol. Continuous if life-threatening. Limit oxygen drive to 6 minutes, then titrate.
They have both, and they are exhausted with a quiet chest Treat as life-threatening asthma. Target 94–98%.

Drugs

Open the airway

  • Salbutamol 5 mg, oxygen-driven nebuliser
  • Repeat it. Continuous nebulised salbutamol is the step before adrenaline
  • Ipratropium 500 micrograms nebulised for severe or life-threatening asthma, or a poor response to salbutamol

Do not delay

  • Steroid now: prednisolone if they can swallow, otherwise hydrocortisone. Use the live dose
  • Intramuscular adrenaline if life-threatening asthma is still deteriorating on continuous salbutamol. Confirm the live dose; the usual adult figure is 500 micrograms
  • Consider magnesium if life-threatening asthma is not improving on continuous salbutamol. Use the live dose

Anaphylaxis is a different first drug: intramuscular adrenaline immediately, not after a trial of salbutamol. Heart failure is a different illness: do not put a wet chest on this pathway.

Check yourself

QuestionAnswer
Oxygen target in acute asthma? 94–98%. 88–92% is the COPD target when they are not critically ill.
The wheeze has gone and the chest is quiet. A silent chest can be pre-terminal. Recovery is easier speech and saturations heading for 94–98%.
COPD and asthma, exhausted, sats 90%, quiet chest. 94–98%, and treat as life-threatening asthma.
Does the 6-minute neb rule apply to pure asthma? No. That sentence is in the COPD guidance.

Pre-alert

Any life-threatening feature, a severe attack that does not turn around, previous near-fatal asthma, or you are giving adrenaline.
